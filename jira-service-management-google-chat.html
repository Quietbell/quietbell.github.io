Jira Service Management alerts in Google Chat

Updated 20 November 2026 · 5 min read

Support teams on Google Workspace want three things in Chat: to see new requests arrive, to know when an SLA is about to slip, and to never, ever post an internal note in a space a customer could read. Here is what works with Jira Service Management (JSM) Cloud today.

What is worth sending

EventWhyWhere
New request createdTriage starts in the space, not in e-mailTeam space
Request moved to Waiting for customer / back to In progressShows who holds the ballTeam space, in the request's thread
Priority raised to HighestPull people in nowTeam space with @all
SLA at risk (for example 30 min before Time to first response breaches)The only alert that saves an SLATeam space, or the assignee's personal space
Public comment from the customerReplies get fasterRequest's thread
Internal notesNever. Chat spaces are shared and searchableNowhere

Three ways to set it up

1. JSM automation + Send web request (free)

JSM automation has the triggers you need: Work item created, SLA threshold breached (choose the SLA and "will breach in 30 minutes"), Work item transitioned. The action is Send web request to a Google Chat webhook, see the card template. Two rules to know: the request is not retried if Google is slow or down, and a rule on Work item commented sends internal notes too unless you add the condition {{comment.internal}} equals false. Test it with a note before you trust it.

2. Google's app for Jira

Free and installed from the Marketplace. It covers project-level notifications and lets people get their own alerts in a direct message with the app. It does not distinguish internal notes from public comments in its settings, so most JSM teams only use it for creation and status events.

3. A dedicated notifications app (Threadbell)

Threadbell treats JSM differently by design:

  • Internal notes and restricted comments are never sent. Not optional, not configurable: the app checks the comment's visibility in Jira before posting.
  • One thread per request, so the whole life of a ticket is readable in one place.
  • Rules per space: priority, status transitions, issue type, and any JQL (request type included) such as "Time to resolution" < remaining("30m") for a digest.
  • Daily digests: "requests waiting on us since more than 24 h" every morning at 9:00.
  • Personal alerts for agents: assigned to me, mentioned, my requests commented.
  • Quiet hours for the on-call space, with one summary when the quiet ends.

Threadbell reads JSM requests through the standard Jira API, with read-only permissions. Agents and customers are never contacted directly: messages only go to the webhooks you configure.

A setup that works for a 5-person support team

  1. Space #support-inbox: new requests, priority raised, customer comments. One thread per request.
  2. Space #support-oncall: only Highest priority and "SLA will breach in 30 min". Quiet hours 20:00–08:00 with a morning summary.
  3. Each agent: personal alerts "assigned to me" and "mentioned", in their own space.
  4. Digest at 9:00 in #support-inbox: open requests older than 2 days, grouped by status.

Frequently asked

Can customers see the Google Chat messages? Only if they are members of the space. Keep support spaces internal, and never post internal notes anywhere.

Does this need a Google Workspace plan? Incoming webhooks require Business or Enterprise, and the admin must allow them.

What about Opsgenie / alerts? JSM operations alerts have their own integrations. This page is about requests (the service desk side).
